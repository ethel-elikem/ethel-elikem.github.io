About & CV

I am a doctoral researcher at the Interdisciplinary Institute for Societal Computing at Saarland University.

My research lies at the intersection of computational social science, urban analytics, and digital platforms. I am interested in how people perceive, experience, and interact with everyday environments, and how these experiences are reflected in user-generated data.

My work uses computational methods to investigate socio-cultural dynamics and patterns of human behaviour in physical spaces. This includes examining how racialisation is expressed in accounts of everyday encounters, understanding patterns in online reviews, and exploring how people perceive and evaluate urban spaces through a social lens.

My broader interests include migration, belonging, and spatial inequality—questions I hope to explore further in future research.

My academic background spans Economics, Mathematics, Mathematical Sciences, and Data Science, which informs my interdisciplinary approach to research.
