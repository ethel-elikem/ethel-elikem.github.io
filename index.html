About

My research lies at the intersection of computational social science, urban analytics, and digital platforms. I am interested in understanding how people perceive, experience, and interact with everyday environments, and how these experiences are reflected in user-generated data.

My academic background spans Economics, Mathematics, Mathematical Sciences, and Data Science, which informs my interdisciplinary approach to research.

Research

My work uses computational methods to investigate socio-cultural dynamics and patterns of human behaviour in physical spaces. This includes examining how racialisation is expressed in accounts of everyday encounters, understanding patterns in online reviews, and exploring how people perceive and evaluate urban spaces through a social lens.

My broader research interests include migration, belonging, and spatial inequality, which I hope to explore further in future work.

Current and recent work

  • Racialisation and everyday encounters: examining accounts of social encounters in public swimming pools in Germany.
  • Online review patterns: studying how people review places and describe their experiences.
  • Perceptions of urban spaces: investigating how urban environments, including greenness, are perceived and evaluated.
